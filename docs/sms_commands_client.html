Get Cellular Info

Send getcellularinfo (case-insensitive) to request fresh cellular diagnostics. The response is sent in separate numbered messages headed CellularInfo part 1, CellularInfo part 2, etc., each at most 160 characters. Read parts in number order; a long field can continue in the next part. If sending fails, the remaining parts stop; resend the command for a new report.

The report includes modem model/firmware, IMEI/ICCID/IMSI, carrier and SIM readiness, registration, raw CSQ and converted RSL, operator/technology, preferred radio modes, configured APN, live data-context states/IPs, cached location, and raw serving-cell information.

CSQ=18,99 converts to RSL=-77. CSQ's first value is the RSSI index; its second value is BER (99 means unknown). RSL is a CSQ-derived dBm estimate. Failed CSQ queries show CSQ=-1,-1 and RSL=Unknown; RSSI code 99 also means unknown. Normal status and alert messages use RSL=-1 for unavailable signal.

CREG 1 means home registration and 5 means roaming. CEREG is the raw LTE registration response. CNMP 38 means LTE only; CMNB 1 means CAT-M, 2 means NB-IoT, and 3 means both. COPS, CNACT, and CPSI retain their modem field order. CPSI includes serving-cell radio mode, network, cell, band, and LTE signal metrics when available. Lat/Lon are cached, not a new GPS fix.

Failed or unsupported diagnostic queries show Unavailable; existing identity/PIN/carrier queries may show Error or Unknown. The command makes read-only queries and does not change carrier, radio modes, or data activation. Timeouts may delay the reply. See the RSL diagnostic notes for interpreting a persistent −77 reading.

Device Power Detection Thresholds

These universal settings apply to all four outlets and are saved across restarts. At startup, power below 1.000 W is off and power at or above 1.000 W is on. During operation, power must rise above the on threshold to change on or fall below the off threshold to change off.

Utility Power Lost — Fuse Probably Blown

When input AC is detected on FUSE_DETECTION (P1.12), but all socket metering channels are below 50 V for three consecutive network-ready alert polls, the utility-loss message includes a probable fuse fault. The optocoupler input is active-low and sampled over up to 40 ms to detect AC pulses. This assumes the optocoupler measures before the fuse and the meters measure after it.

Output
Alert=Utility power lost - fuse probably blown
Utility=OFF
Device=0110
Outlet=1111
RSL=-101
Batt=4.44
Lat=42.027998
Lon=-91.631620
FW=001.00
HBD=0.000

No incoming command is required and no outlet report follows this alert. Without detected input AC, the normal utility-loss alert remains. If input AC returns during an outage but socket voltage does not, this alert follows after confirmation. A metering fault or another downstream interruption can also cause these symptoms. Failed sends are retried; a successful fuse alert is sent once until socket voltage returns, when the existing restoration alert and outlet report follow.

Device Power Alerts

The monitor averages five active-power readings independently per outlet, uses the universal on/off thresholds, and confirms a change for three consecutive averaged readings before sending an alert. Startup uses 1.000 W and initializes silently. Negative active-power readings are reported as zero. Intentional outlet-off periods reset the monitoring baseline and do not generate these alerts.

Output
Alert=Device power lost
Outlet=3
3=119.340V,0.000A,0.000W
 
Alert=Device power restored
Outlet=3
3=119.340V,0.035A,4.262W
